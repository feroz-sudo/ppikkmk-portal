The Master of Counseling (Clinical Mental Health) (CMHC) is a 63-credit-hour program designed
to prepare students to become licensed professional mental health counselors. The curriculum
is structured based on the Training Standards and Qualifications for Counselors with a Major
Focus Area by the Malaysian Board of Counselors (2016) and the Council for Accreditation of
Counseling and Related Education Programs (CACREP). This program integrates theory and
clinical training across different settings:
30% classroom learning
40% laboratory training
30% workplace/industry training
In line with the standards of the Malaysian Board of Counselors, CACREP, and the university's
training objectives, the KKMK program prepares students to work with diverse populations in
various settings. The practicum and internship experiences represent the culmination of a
counselor’s education, where trainees apply theoretical knowledge and counseling skills
directly in real-world settings, producing knowledgeable, skilled, and professional clinical
mental health counselors who meet the national workforce needs.
1.1 Internship
The internship is a supervised post-practicum clinical experience aimed at refining, enhancing,
and strengthening the application of counseling knowledge and skills in professional training
settings (Malaysian Board of Counselors Training Standards and Qualifications, 2016). This
clinical experience includes direct mental health counseling as well as other activities related to
clinical mental health counseling.
According to CACREP (Section 3: Professional Practice, 2016), both practicum and internship are
professional training experiences that facilitate the application of counseling theories and skill
development under supervision. This experience provides students with the opportunity to
conduct counseling sessions with clients representing diverse ethnic and demographic
backgrounds within their communities.

1.2 Internship Objectives
The primary objective of the internship is to provide hands-on training to counselor trainees
by offering high-quality supervised professional counseling experiences consistent with the
academic preparation, university/program goals, and career objectives of the counselor
trainees. This experience aims to help counselor trainees develop competencies by applying
theoretical knowledge and integrating clinical skills under the supervision of experienced and
qualified professionals.
2.0 Clinical Mental Health Counseling Internship (KKP60306)
The Clinical Mental Health Counseling Internship (KKP60306) at UPSI provides trainees with
opportunities to engage in clinical training in mental health settings, focusing on:
Case conceptualization
Case formulation
Diagnostics
Treatment planning
Interventions
Trainees will receive clinical supervision from both academic and field supervisors who are
professionals in the mental health field. In accordance with the Malaysian Board of
Counselors' qualification standards, trainees must complete a total of 504 internship hours,
which include:
192 direct face-to-face counseling service hours
112 hours of professional counselor-related activities
200 hours of case management and administrative work
The internship process involves hands-on learning, experiential training, didactic instruction,
and case presentations, allowing trainees to refine, enhance, and strengthen their application
of knowledge, skills, and professional counseling practices in an ethical and culturally
competent manner.

At the end of the internship course, trainees are expected to be able to:
Apply theories, principles, and practices of assessment, diagnosis, intervention,
referral, and prevention of mental and emotional disorders in individual and group
counseling sessions.
Conceptualize mental health diagnoses using DSM-5-TR based on background
information, assessments, clinical interviews, and relevant informant reports.
Promote human development, well-being, and optimal mental health through
prevention, education, and advocacy activities for career development in clinical
mental health counseling.
Select appropriate assessment and intervention techniques to facilitate diagnosis,
treatment planning, and effective case management.
Adhere to the ethical standards of the Malaysian Board of Counselors, as well as
national laws and guidelines for mental health services in Malaysia.
